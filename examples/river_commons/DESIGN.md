# Art direction: River Commons

## Brief

Audience: a district-design workshop. Mode: live presentation with editable source for subsequent discussion. Takeaway: treat the water boundary as a public-space relationship, and start with a small test that has an operating owner. This is a fictional concept; photographs are reference material.

## Three structural hypotheses

1. **Landscape edge.** Broad contours meet city photography; serif statements act as stable reading surfaces. Fit: boundaries directly express the subject. Risk: repetitive left-text/right-photo silhouettes.
2. **Aperture atlas.** Offset windows reveal local details against generous paper space; maps and annotations support inquiry. Fit: moves from district to block. Risk: crops could conceal the subject.
3. **Evidence layers.** Translucent annotations over full-slide photographs, compact sans typography, and dense spatial analysis. Fit: supports real field observations. Risk: insufficient source evidence and fragile export transparency.

Selection: combine landscape edges and apertures. Use opaque surfaces for legibility and reliable Office output. Avoid transparency where it cannot carry useful information. Retain plain slides for the chart and operating questions.

## Flexible design DNA

Visual thesis: the same edge that separates land and water can connect a public-space story.

- Identity: broad single curves, quiet paper fields, rust-colored boundary accents, forest-green reading surfaces.
- Type: Georgia bold display, Arial body; 24–26 pt body, 44–64 pt display, 12–14 pt attribution and metadata.
- Space: 48 pt primary inset; generous line spacing; deliberately varying title and evidence relationships.
- Material: opaque paper over photography; independent SVG apertures and contours; no blur, bevel, or generic glass.
- Color: deep green establishes stable reading surfaces, pale blue identifies water, rust marks attention and inquiry.
- Images: authentic local references, never passed off as project documentation.
- Data: qualitative, explicitly unmeasured; native editable marks and labels.
- Variation: vertical reveal, enclosed aperture, annotation panes, sectional strata, connecting ribbon, diagonal corridor, horizontal cutout, and two plain layouts.
- Avoid: generic icon grids, three decorative cards, repetitive split layouts, fake numerical evidence, photo tinting that hides content.

## Production and critique

Opening, sectional explanation, and qualitative-chart prototypes were rendered before the full-deck export. Final review checks every slide, plus full-size photo masks, annotation placement, label clearance, contrast, and export layering. Native text is never flattened into an image.

Originality checks: broad river contours are subject-specific rather than decorative template swooshes; the technical sketch deliberately omits overlays; aperture orientation and scale change with the story; silhouettes vary across the sequence.

Limit: organic paths are separate SVG artwork in PowerPoint, not native freeform vertices. Their original SVG files remain editable. The photographs and contour surfaces remain separate layers.
